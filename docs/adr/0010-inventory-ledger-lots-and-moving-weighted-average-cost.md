# ADR 0010: Inventory Ledger with Lots and Moving Weighted Average Cost

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-11-05
- **Author:** Jorge Armando Ceras Cárdenas - [armandx06](https://github.com/armandx06)

---

## Context and Problem Description

The business needs to control stock while preserving shelf life and cost information. A simple quantity per product is not enough: it cannot answer which batch expires first, what a batch cost, or why the average cost changed.

The pre-implementation model had an `inventory_batches` table with `quantity`, `unit_cost_snapshot`, and `entry_date`, but no remaining quantity, expiration date, movement ledger, or cost history. It also had no operational purchases, sales, or production tables.

The first inventory iteration must support:

- Stock on hand per product.
- Lots with received quantity, remaining quantity, expiration date, and actual unit cost.
- An immutable history of every stock change.
- Moving weighted average cost (WAC) for valuation and cost of goods sold.
- FEFO consumption so the oldest or closest-to-expiry stock leaves first.
- Negative stock allowed with a warning, to be reviewed later.
- Future purchases, recipes, production, and sales without redesigning the core.

The business currently operates from one location. The model still records a location so that adding a second one later does not require changing every inventory table.

## Decision Drivers

- Shelf life and FEFO must be first-class, not an afterthought.
- COGS must be stable and independent of which lot the system happens to pick.
- Every stock change must be auditable and reconstructable.
- Current balances must be fast to query.
- Future purchases, sales, and production must post through the same ledger.
- The MVP must avoid a full warehouse-management system.
- Concurrency must not corrupt balances or average costs.

## Options Considered

1. **Single mutable quantity per product:** one row with `quantity`, `unit_cost`, and no lot history.
2. **FIFO/FEFO with actual lot cost:** each issue uses the actual cost of the lots consumed; the average is only a report.
3. **Moving weighted average plus a lot subledger:** WAC drives valuation and COGS; lots drive quantity, expiry, and traceability.
4. **WAC plus a separate cost-history table:** same as option 3 but with an additional table dedicated to cost changes.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: **Option 3, moving weighted average with a lot subledger.**

The inventory core has four tables:

```text
locations
inventory_lots
inventory_movements
stock_balances
```

`locations` starts with a single default row, `Principal`. Lots, movements, and balances reference it.

### Lot identity

Each lot has two identifiers:

- `system_lot_code`: generated by Sweetboard, unique, for example `LOT-20261105-000042`.
- `manufacturer_lot_code`: optional, supplied by the vendor or by the production batch.

This keeps internal traceability independent from supplier numbering and still preserves the manufacturer's code.

### Lots

```text
inventory_lots
├── id                    uuid primary key
├── product_id            uuid, references products
├── location_id           uuid, references locations
├── system_lot_code       varchar, unique, generated
├── manufacturer_lot_code varchar, nullable
├── received_at           timestamptz
├── manufactured_at       date, nullable
├── expiration_date       date, nullable
├── quantity_received     numeric, greater than zero
├── quantity_remaining    numeric, greater than or equal to zero
├── unit_cost             numeric, actual cost of this lot
├── status                available | quarantine | blocked | written_off
├── source_type           initial | manual | purchase | production | return
├── source_id             uuid, nullable
├── notes                 text, nullable
├── created_by            uuid, nullable, references employees
└── created_at            timestamptz
```

`unit_cost` is retained for traceability and supplier analysis. Under WAC it is not used to value an issue; it is used to calculate the new average when the lot enters.

Manual entries always create a new lot. The system never edits a lot's `quantity_received` or `unit_cost` after creation. `quantity_remaining` and `status` change as stock moves or is written off.

### Movements

`inventory_movements` is an append-only ledger. Rows are never updated or deleted. Corrections are new movements that offset the original one.

```text
inventory_movements
├── id                    uuid primary key
├── movement_seq          bigint identity, unique, ledger order
├── product_id            uuid, references products
├── location_id           uuid, references locations
├── lot_id                uuid, nullable, references inventory_lots
├── movement_type         varchar
├── quantity_delta        numeric, signed
├── unit_cost             numeric, frozen at posting time
├── total_cost            numeric, same sign as quantity_delta
├── quantity_after        numeric
├── inventory_value_after numeric
├── average_cost_after    numeric
├── occurred_at           timestamptz, business date
├── posted_at             timestamptz, default now()
├── source_type           initial | manual | purchase | sale | production
├── source_id             uuid, nullable
├── notes                 text, nullable
├── created_by            uuid, nullable, references employees
└── created_at            timestamptz
```

Storing the after-state on each movement turns the ledger into a complete cost history without a separate table. `occurred_at` records when the business event happened; `posted_at` records when it entered the system. `movement_seq` is the authoritative ledger order because PostgreSQL timestamps are transaction-scoped and can tie.

Movement types planned for the full system:

```text
initial_stock
adjustment_in
adjustment_out
expiry_writeoff
purchase_receipt
purchase_return
sale_issue
sale_return
production_consumption
production_output
transfer_in
transfer_out
```

The first iteration exposes only `initial_stock`, `adjustment_in`, `adjustment_out`, and `expiry_writeoff`.

`lot_id` is nullable because an issue that produces negative stock has no physical lot for the shortfall. FEFO covers the lots that exist; the remainder is posted without a lot.

### Balances

```text
stock_balances
├── product_id        uuid, references products
├── location_id       uuid, references locations
├── quantity_on_hand  numeric, may be negative
├── inventory_value   numeric, never negative
├── average_cost      numeric, never negative
├── last_cost         numeric, never negative
├── last_movement_at  timestamptz, nullable
└── updated_at        timestamptz
```

Primary key: `(product_id, location_id)`. WAC is maintained per product and location. With one location this is equivalent to a single average per product; if more locations are added, transfers will move cost between them.

`stock_balances` is a cache. It can be rebuilt from `inventory_movements`.

### Costing policy

Let `q` be the quantity on hand, `v` the inventory value, `avg` the current average cost, and `last` the last known receipt cost.

**Inbound** (`d > 0` at unit cost `c`):

```python
last = c

if q <= 0:
    new_q = q + d
    if new_q > 0:
        v = new_q * c
        avg = c
    else:
        v = 0
        avg = c
else:
    v = v + d * c
    new_q = q + d
    avg = v / new_q
```

**Outbound** (`d < 0`, with `s = -d`):

```python
if q > 0:
    from_stock = min(s, q)
    shortfall = s - from_stock
    issue_cost = from_stock * avg + shortfall * last
else:
    issue_cost = s * last

unit_cost = issue_cost / s
total_cost = -issue_cost
new_q = q - s

if new_q >= 0:
    v = max(0, v - from_stock * avg)
else:
    v = 0

if new_q <= 0:
    avg = last
```

This handles a sale or adjustment that crosses from positive to negative stock: the covered quantity leaves at WAC and the shortfall leaves at the last known cost.

Additional rules:

- When stock reaches zero, `inventory_value = 0` and `average_cost` remains the last known cost.
- `inventory_value` never becomes negative.
- Negative stock is allowed and must return a warning. It appears in the inventory review report.
- `last_cost` is updated on every inbound and is the fallback when there is no positive stock.
- `expiry_writeoff` cannot exceed the remaining quantity of its lot and never creates negative stock.
- `adjustment_out` may create negative stock, but it requires a note.
- All calculations use `Decimal`, never floating point. Values are stored with six decimals and displayed with two for money.
- Before changing a balance, the transaction locks the `stock_balances` row with `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`; lots selected by FEFO are locked as well.
- For future purchases, sales, and production, a uniqueness rule on `(source_type, source_id, movement_type, product_id, lot_id)` with `NULLS NOT DISTINCT` prevents posting the same source twice, including lotless movements.

### FEFO and expiry

- Automatic consumption orders lots by `expiration_date ASC NULLS LAST, received_at ASC`.
- Only lots with `status = 'available'` and a non-expired `expiration_date` are consumed automatically.
- Expired lots are not consumed silently. They are written off or blocked.
- A write-off reduces `quantity_remaining`; when it reaches zero the lot becomes `written_off`.
- Alerts cover lots expiring within a configurable window, expired lots with stock, products at or below `minimum_stock`, and products with negative stock.

### Minimum stock

Each product carries:

- `minimum_stock`: alert threshold.
- `target_stock`: desired level after replenishment.

The first iteration only reports low stock. Purchase lists grouped by supplier and production suggestions are later iterations; they will consume the same balances, lots, minimums, and expiry data.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Every stock change is auditable and the balance can be reconstructed.
- Shelf life and FEFO are supported from the first iteration.
- WAC gives stable COGS and a stable basis for pricing.
- Lots preserve actual costs for supplier and production analysis.
- Purchases, recipes, production, and sales can post through the same ledger later.
- Negative stock is visible instead of hidden.

### Negative / Trade-offs

- More tables and rules than a single stock column.
- WAC is not exact lot costing; the remaining book value will not equal the sum of remaining lot costs after issues.
- Per-location WAC will require an explicit transfer policy when a second location appears.
- Negative stock needs an operational review process, not only a warning.
- FEFO requires locking and careful ordering under concurrency.
- The ledger grows continuously; reporting and archival strategy will be needed eventually.
